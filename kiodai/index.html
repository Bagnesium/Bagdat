Can AI agents actually remember what they're supposed to do later?

Bagdat Beimzhan · Independent research · 2026

read the paper (PDF, 11 pages)

the idea

I wanted to figure out what happens when you tell an AI agent to do something later, then change the deadline, cancel the task, or make it depend on something else happening first.

This is called prospective memory. Basically, remembering to do something in the future, not just remembering information from the past.

My first prompting experiments didn't show any improvement, so I tested three different setups using DeepSeek V3.1: a regular agent without external intention storage (A0), one with a structured memory ledger it could query (B_ledger), and one with programmatic monitoring (A2).

I compared them across 12 synthetic task sequences from four template families, running each setup once per sequence.

what actually happened

The simplest setup came out on top.

The baseline scored 0.8643 in mean trajectory Set-F1, compared with 0.8111 for the ledger system and 0.4806 for the monitored system. The baseline was also the cheapest in API-reported costs.

So, the more complicated memory systems actually performed worse.

That doesn't mean structured memory is a bad idea. This was a small experiment using one model configuration, with scenarios I'd already worked with during development. It just means my implementations didn't outperform the baseline under these conditions.

what i took away

The most interesting part was looking through the failures.

Old deadlines would resurface after being replaced. Actions sometimes got linked to the wrong instructions. In other cases, the agent had the information it needed but still picked the wrong action.

That made me realize something: storing an instruction and actually following through on it are two different problems.

A memory system can store perfectly valid data and still get the meaning wrong somewhere along the way.

The experiment didn't isolate exactly why the more complex systems struggled. But it gave me a much clearer direction for investigating the problem: follow an intention from when it's first recorded, through every update or cancellation, all the way to the final action.
